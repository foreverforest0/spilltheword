# Ellipsis & substitution · disappearing text

Reference: https://recent.design/i/t3hk5ld-scroll-text-disintegration-effect
Repeated words soften, disperse as text-shaped canvas dust and disappear in
place. Native page scroll drives progress when animation is enabled; Hide
repetition / Restore words also works without scrolling. Canvas particles are
sampled from the actual glyphs; no image assets or animation library is used.
The reference's black surface is replaced with the user's explicit cream site
background, accented Kindaten headings and level colours.

There are 24 original dialogues at each of B2 and C1. Each has a complete,
grammatical reply, identified repeated material, a clear shortening task, a
concealed hint and an example. Contexts cover films, social media, digital habits,
friends, food, trips, hobbies, smart-home mishaps and personal decisions, with
little workplace content. Only the actual approved B2 ALH list supplies lexical
metadata. C1 uses this approved pool until a separate C1 list is supplied.

Six filters focus on verb ellipsis, infinitive ellipsis, noun substitution,
so/not, do so and so/neither agreement; All patterns mixes them. Coverage includes
one/ones, determiner-head ellipsis (some/most/all), finite and modal auxiliaries,
perfect/continuous/passive reference, final to, if so/not, negative scope with
hope/assume, matching auxiliaries in agreement, and C1 coordination gapping/stripping. Do so is used for actions,
not states. A perfect chain is retained where the task explicitly requests it;
other grammatical reductions get neutral review rather than false rejection.

The approach follows standard distinctions taught in Cambridge's English
Grammar in Use / Advanced Grammar in Use, Oxford Practice Grammar and Pearson
MyGrammarLab. Sentences are original; no published exercises are reproduced or
specific publisher pages claimed as retrieved.

Written checking accepts documented alternatives and contractions, and gives
precise feedback for a small set of known errors. Other short replies receive
neutral comparison with an example, not an unsupported verdict. Oral practice
always works via Show example without a score or fake checked answer. Drafts,
examples and feedback persist per task; filters keep those drafts intact.

The complete original reply remains available to assistive technology even
while its visual repetition disappears. Restore is always available. Motion
reduction disables animated dust and scroll effects while keeping instant
button control. Task changes return smoothly to the dialogue, without trapping
native scroll or focusing a mobile keyboard.
