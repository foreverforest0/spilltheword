# Weekly future calendar proposal

Printer: only will and Present Simple timetables once the calendar is implemented.
Calendar: going to, Present Continuous arrangements, Future Continuous, Future Perfect,
Future Perfect Continuous and Mix. Keep the lift as a separate practice.

## Variant A — one person's week
A real Monday–Sunday grid, appointments already entered with start/end times.
Each event has a name, activity and status: intention / confirmed. Use B1 course vocabulary.
No future target sentence is prewritten inside the calendar.
Example entries:
- Monday: Oliver · work a shift · 09:00–13:00 · confirmed
- Tuesday: Amelia · meet the manager · 10:00–11:00 · confirmed
- Wednesday: Harry · watch a documentary · 18:00–19:30 · booked
- Thursday: Florence · write a script · 14:00–17:00 · planned
- Friday: George · apply for a part-time job · 10:00–11:00 · intention
- Saturday: Charlotte · meet a friend · 12:00–14:00 · confirmed
- Sunday: Arthur · call a friend to stay in touch · 17:00–17:30 · agreed
Use one named owner per week in this variant, rather than all seven names in one week;
these entries illustrate the pool of activities and names for different weeks.

## Variant B — a shared week
Same weekly grid but appointments belong to several named people. Names and clear
colours distinguish them. Same time ranges, planned/confirmed status and two modes.
Learner can compare simultaneous activities without invented hidden facts.

## Two training modes inside either variant
1. Questions: Rough Notation circles an event, task appears to the right. One task per
selection. Relevant time is visible in the question, not an extra sidebar.
   - Shift 09:00–13:00: "What is Oliver doing at 11 on Monday? Describe his future activity."
     Model: "Oliver will be working a shift at eleven on Monday."
   - Same shift: "Say what Oliver will have finished by 2 pm on Monday."
     Model: "Oliver will have finished his shift by two on Monday."
   - Same shift: "How long will Oliver have been working by noon on Monday?"
     Model: "By noon on Monday, Oliver will have been working for three hours."
   - Confirmed meeting: "Describe Amelia's arrangement for Tuesday."
     Model: "Amelia is meeting her manager on Tuesday."
2. No questions: only the annotated event and its facts. Learner chooses a suitable
future form and a reference time, then says/writes one sentence. Hint gives a semantic
reminder; Show answers lists multiple valid perspectives. No automatic grading.

Cancellation: a single Cancel event action strikes through the selected event with
Rough Notation. The label changes to Cancelled. Same appointment stays visible.
For Questions, negative task replaces the current task and previous revealed model
is hidden. For No questions, learner explains the changed plan. Restore reverses it.
In Future Perfect cancellation, use "will not have done ... by ..." only if the revised
facts establish non-completion by the reference point. Cancellation alone does not
prove that an action cannot happen later; avoid logically forced wrong models.

Always show Hint and Show answers; preserve notes separately by mode, tense and event.
Don't show the selected tense in the Mix task. A same event may support multiple forms;
accept meaningfully different answers and don't pretend the calendar determines one.
